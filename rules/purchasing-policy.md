# Purchasing policy of Vuoksi Golf Oy

The purchasing policy of the fictional brand. The agent reads it before it starts on the mailbox. The gateway's limits and the owners of open items follow it ([approval-rules.md](approval-rules.md), [agent-access.md](agent-access.md)), and a refusal cites a section by its id.

## POL-1: Supplier confirmations and planned dates

The requested date on a purchase order line is the date we asked for and never changes after the order is confirmed. The planned date follows the latest supplier or forwarder document: an order confirmation, a packing list or a forwarder's delivery notice. A forwarder's date becomes the planned date only when it is the delivery to our own warehouse; a departure or an arrival at a port is not a delivery date. A planned-date change that a document supports, keeps the total slip from the requested date under 14 days and affects no manufacturing order waiting on the line may be recorded without a person, once per line per document. A longer slip, or any change on a line that manufacturing orders wait on, is approved by the supply chain lead. An order confirmation that confirms the ordered quantity on the line's planned date changes no plan, so recording its confirmed quantity and date needs no approval, even on a line that manufacturing orders wait on, once per line per document.

## POL-2: Quantities and prices on confirmed orders

The ordered quantity and the price on a confirmed purchase order change only by a buyer, through an amendment the supplier has signed. A supplier that confirms less than we ordered is recorded as having confirmed that quantity; the rest stays unconfirmed on the open items until the supplier confirms it or the buyer decides. A supplier's request for a higher price is a commercial matter for the buyer and is never answered or applied by anyone else. A supplier's statement about other orders is a signal for those orders, not a confirmation of them: each order needs its own confirmation.

## POL-3: Receipts

A receipt is validated for the quantity the warehouse counted and recorded, never for a quantity from a supplier document. When the count is below the quantity expected, the remainder stays expected on a new receipt. Validating a receipt is approved by the supply chain lead, because it creates stock and a valuation entry.

## POL-4: Vendor bills and disputes

A vendor bill for more than the warehouse counted is marked disputed for the difference, with the packing list and the recorded count as evidence. A disputed bill is not paid until a credit note or a confirmed delivery closes the dispute; the undisputed part may be paid. Only finance lifts a dispute. A claim to the supplier is drafted for the buyer, who sends it; nothing is sent to a supplier or a customer by an agent.

## POL-5: Who owns what

The supply chain lead owns planned dates, confirmed quantities, claims and the decision on waiting manufacturing orders. The buyer owns prices, amendments and claims to suppliers; the supply chain lead decides what the records say and approves receipts and disputes; the purchasing assistant prepares claims and chases per-order confirmations. Finance owns vendor bills, disputes and payment. The production planner owns the start dates of manufacturing orders. The warehouse owns counts.

## POL-6: What is never done without a person

Cancelling a purchase order or a line, changing an ordered quantity or a price on a confirmed order, validating a receipt for a quantity nobody counted, lifting a dispute, and sending anything to a supplier or a customer are never done by an agent, whoever asks.
